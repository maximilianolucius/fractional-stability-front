## Adversarial assessment

The round materially narrows the structured-matrix opportunity.

Classical D-stability already contains exact low-dimensional, tridiagonal and acyclic results; Metzler matrices possess strong diagonal-stability/Hurwitz structure; qualitative sign stability is graph-theoretically mature; and general D-stability has known computational-hardness evidence. Combined with Kushel–Pavani's conic/LMI-region theory, this makes a generic “ecological structured D-stability” program too easy to overclaim.

The only credible residual problem is a **class-specific conic-sector multiplicative D-stability theorem** for a biologically natural cyclic, non-Metzler matrix family not reducible to known acyclic/tridiagonal/positive classes.

That is a legitimate mathematical possibility, but no such biologically compelled class has yet emerged from the Double Allee analysis. Therefore it is not promoted as the primary branch.

## Evidence accepted

- exact D-stability criteria exist in dimensions 2–4;
- real tridiagonal D-stability has a necessary-and-sufficient characterization;
- acyclic D-stable matrices have a necessary-and-sufficient characterization;
- Metzler/Hurwitz structure collapses several stability questions and gives diagonal certificates;
- general D-stability has a structured-singular-value characterization and strong hardness evidence;
- qualitative/sign stability has mature graph-theoretic characterizations;
- none of these automatically supplies a prescribed conic angular margin.

Do not start from a desired matrix theorem and retrofit ecology to it.

1. derive the mechanism-resolved Double Allee model;
2. inspect its Jacobian family;
3. only if that family is structurally distinctive, revisit conic D-stability.
