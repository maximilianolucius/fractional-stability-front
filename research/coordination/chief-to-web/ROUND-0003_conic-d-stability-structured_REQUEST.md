## Objective

Determine whether the remaining structured-matrix frontier around **conic-sector multiplicative D-stability** is genuinely open after accounting for generalized regional D-stability.

The target is no longer the existence of fractional/conic D-stability theory. That broad idea is already covered.

The target is:

> exact, finite, structure-sensitive characterizations of conic-sector multiplicative D-stability for meaningful matrix classes.

## Exact questions

1. Are there necessary-and-sufficient, finite, quantifier-free criteria for conic-sector/relative D-stability in:
   - arbitrary dimension;
   - dimensions 2 or 3;
   - Metzler/positive classes;
   - sign/qualitative matrix classes;
   - diagonally dominant classes;
   - P-matrix/M-matrix-related classes;
   - graph-sparse matrices;
   - ecological/community matrices?
2. What complexity/hardness results exist for:
   - classical D-stability;
   - generalized/regional D-stability;
   - conic-sector D-stability;
   - checking diagonal stability?
3. Which sign patterns or graph structures guarantee or preclude D-stability?
4. What exact implications/equivalences are known among:
   - diagonal Lyapunov stability;
   - multiplicative D-stability;
   - total stability;
   - sign stability;
   - P-matrix properties;
   - H-matrix/generalized diagonal dominance;
   - sector stability?
5. Does existing qualitative D-stability theory already imply a conic-sector result after rotation/lifting/transformation?
6. Are there exact results specifically for Jacobians of ecological interaction networks, Lotka–Volterra systems, compartmental systems, or positive systems?
7. Does the additive-compound formulation of Kushel–Pavani yield tractable exact tests on recognizable structural subclasses?
8. What are the strongest counterexamples showing that natural sufficient conditions are not necessary?

## Working hypothesis to challenge

A potentially useful gap remains:

> for some ecologically natural sign/graph/Jacobian class, conic-sector multiplicative D-stability may admit an exact finite characterization sharper than the general universally quantified framework.

Try to destroy this hypothesis.
