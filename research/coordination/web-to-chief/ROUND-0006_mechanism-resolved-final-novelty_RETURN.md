## Verdict 3 — Factorization non-identifiability

**Decision category:** **COVERED AFTER REFORMULATION.**

Yates–Evans–Chappell (2009), DOI 10.1016/j.automatica.2009.07.009, provides the strongest conceptual prior theorem: output-preserving Lie symmetries generate transformations preserving model observations and are equivalent to established structural-identifiability transformation methods.

Massonis–Villaverde (2020), DOI 10.3390/sym12030469, systematizes detecting and breaking these symmetries.

Identifiable-combination theory (e.g. Meshkat–Anderson–DiStefano 2011, DOI 10.1016/j.mbs.2011.06.001) establishes that individual parameters can be unidentifiable while combinations entering the input–output equations are identifiable.

The proposed functional transformation

### Nonparametric distinction

The searched structural-identifiability literature is mostly finite-dimensional/parametric. No direct ecological theorem describing an ((m-1))-**function** gauge for unrestricted density-dependent vital-rate functions was found.

Nevertheless, the frozen nonparametric factor ambiguity follows immediately from the product representation and is an instance of standard symmetry logic. It should be presented as a mechanism-specific impossibility corollary, not as a new general identifiability principle.

## Verdict 4 — Minimal observation design

**Decision category:** **COVERED AFTER REFORMULATION.**

Joubert–Stigter–Molenaar (2018), DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0207334, explicitly studies and computes minimal model-output sets whose measurement guarantees structural identifiability.

Integrated population models provide the ecological analogue. Riecke et al. (2019), DOI 10.1111/2041-210X.13195, reviews joint inference from abundance and demographic data streams, including survival/reproduction information that cannot be cleanly inferred from a single aggregate series.

For the unrestricted product model, observing (m-1) independent component functions plus (P=prod A_i) algebraically determines the remaining factor. This is useful but not deep by itself.

A genuinely nontrivial theorem would need realistic observation operators—mark–recapture survival, breeding counts, censoring, latent demographic states, imperfect detection, partial density ranges—rather than direct noiseless observation (y_i=A_i(x)).
