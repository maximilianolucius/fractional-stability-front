### Question investigated

Adversarial audit of the surviving basin-memory candidate:

> In multidimensional strong/Double-Allee Caputo systems, can survival/extinction be a property of a physically reachable memory state rather than of the current population vector alone, with two reachable states having the same present population but potentially belonging to different asymptotic basins?

The round separates the ambient Volterra state space from the states generated by standard finite-terminal Caputo IVPs, then tests classical hereditary/Volterra theory, diffusive/Markovian lifts, monotone-system restrictions, and direct bistable-memory prior art.

## Executive finding

ROUND-0012 materially narrows, but does not eliminate, the candidate.

Two claims that were still partly entangled in ROUND-0011 must now be separated.

1. **Same present, different reachable memory is not an ambient-space artifact.**  
   Cong–Tuan's explicit higher-dimensional Caputo counterexample provides standard constant-initial-data IVPs whose physical trajectories meet at finite time. Combining this with the Doan–Kloeden continuation-state construction shows that the evaluation map from the physically reachable memory set to current physical state is non-injective in general dimension \(d\ge2\).

2. **Same present, opposite asymptotic basins inside the physically reachable set remains unresolved in the searched corpus.**  
   No theorem or explicit autonomous bistable Caputo example satisfying all of:
   - standard finite-terminal Caputo IVPs;
   - two distinct reachable memory states;
   - identical current physical vector;
   - different future trajectories;
   - convergence to different attractors,
   
   was located.

A major 2026 novelty threat was found: Khalighi et al., arXiv:2602.20365, studies bistable fractional systems with history-dependent stability landscapes, delayed collapse/recovery, rollback, and memory-broadened hysteresis, and explicitly discusses distinct histories that can momentarily share a physical state. This substantially weakens any novelty claim based merely on “memory makes stability landscapes/history-dependent tipping depend on the past.” It does **not** provide the strict reachable-same-present/opposite-basin theorem requested here.

Classical Volterra/minimal-state theory and modern infinite-state/diffusive representations strongly subsume the **need for an enlarged state**. They do not, in the searched sources, subsume the exact basin-fiber question on the Caputo reachable set.

# A. Doan–Kloeden state-space interpretation

Primary source:

Thai Son Doan and Peter E. Kloeden, “Semi-Dynamical Systems Generated by Autonomous Caputo Fractional Differential Equations,” Vietnam Journal of Mathematics 49 (2021), 1305–1315, DOI 10.1007/s10013-020-00464-6.

For

\[
{}^C D^\alpha x(t)=g(x(t)),\qquad 0<\alpha<1,
\]

the standard IVP is equivalent to

\[
x(t)=x_0+
\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1}g(x(s))\,ds.
\]

Doan–Kloeden embed this in the more general Volterra form

\[
x(t)=f(t)+\int_0^t a(t,s)g(x(s))\,ds
\]

on

\[
\mathfrak C=C(\mathbb R_+,\mathbb R^d)
\]

with the compact-open topology.

The semigroup state is the forcing/continuation function \(f\), not merely the current vector \(x(t)\). Their transition operator is

\[
(T_\tau f)(\theta)
=
f(\tau+\theta)
+
\int_0^\tau a(\tau+\theta,s)g(x_f(s))\,ds.
\]

For an ordinary Caputo IVP, the canonical embedding is the constant function

\[
\iota(x_0)(\theta)=x_0.
\]

The current physical state is the evaluation

\[
e_0(f)=f(0),
\qquad
e_0(T_t\iota(x_0))=x(t;x_0).
\]

Define the physical reachable set

\[
\mathcal R_\alpha
=
\left\{
T_t\iota(x_0):
t\ge0,\;x_0\in X_{\rm phys}
\right\}.
\]

### What follows rigorously from the semigroup construction

- \(\iota(X_{\rm phys})\) is the **initial physical slice**.
- It is generally **not invariant**: for a non-equilibrium physical IVP, \(T_t\iota(x_0)\) is normally nonconstant as a function of \(\theta\).
- \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) is forward invariant:
  \[
  T_s\mathcal R_\alpha\subseteq\mathcal R_\alpha
  \]
  by the semigroup property.
- Since each \(T_s\) is continuous, \(\overline{\mathcal R_\alpha}\) is also forward invariant.
- The whole ambient space \(\mathfrak C\) is much larger than \(\mathcal R_\alpha\); arbitrary \(f\in\mathfrak C\) must not be called a physically realized Caputo history without further qualification.
- No inspected source gives a useful intrinsic theorem that \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) itself is closed, a finite-dimensional manifold, or has a specified smooth dimension.

Cui–Kloeden–Xin (arXiv:2607.05799, 2026) substantially strengthens the asymptotic theory on the ambient Volterra semiflow: under dissipativity assumptions they construct a compact absorbing set and attractor and show the attractor consists of equi globally Hölder-continuous functions. This gives strong regularity/compactness information for the asymptotic ambient dynamics, but is not a structural characterization of \(\mathcal R_\alpha\).

### Verdict 1 — physical embedding invariance

**DIRECT PRIOR.**

The constant physical embedding is canonical but generally not invariant. The physically meaningful invariant object after initialization is the orbit saturation \(\mathcal R_\alpha\), not the constant slice alone.

### Verdict 2 — reachable-state structure

**SUBSTANTIAL PARTIAL THEORY.**

Exact transition-state formula, continuity, forward invariance, and modern attractor regularity exist. No searched theorem characterizes \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) as a closed/smooth/minimal manifold or gives a complete basin theory restricted to it.

# B. Same-present / different-memory states

The decisive source is:

N. D. Cong and H. T. Tuan, “Generation of nonlocal fractional dynamical systems by fractional differential equations,” Journal of Integral Equations and Applications 29 (2017), 585–608, DOI 10.1216/JIE-2017-29-4-585.

Their scalar result proves nonintersection. Their higher-dimensional result goes the other way: for \(d\ge2\) they construct a linear autonomous Caputo system with distinct standard IVPs whose trajectories intersect at a finite time. In the two-dimensional construction, a matrix is chosen from a complex zero of a Mittag-Leffler function so that all solutions meet at the origin at a finite time \(T\).

This gives more than an abstract ambient-space collision.

Take two different physical initial states \(x_{01}\ne x_{02}\). Then

\[
e_0(T_T\iota(x_{01}))
=
x(T;x_{01})
=
x(T;x_{02})
=
e_0(T_T\iota(x_{02})).
\]

The two continuation states cannot be equal in general: if

\[
T_T\iota(x_{01})=T_T\iota(x_{02}),
\]

uniqueness of the restarted Volterra problem would force identical continuation, contradicting the fact that the original solutions are distinct functions.

Hence the current-state projection

\[
e_0:\mathcal R_\alpha\to X_{\rm phys}
\]

is **not injective in general for \(d\ge2\)**.

This answers B1 and B2 at theorem level:

- two distinct states in the **physically reachable** set can have the same current vector;
- their future physical trajectories can differ.

It does **not** answer B3: the linear example does not furnish two stable asymptotic outcomes on opposite basins.

### Verdict 3 — same-present different-memory possibility

**DIRECT PRIOR.**

The phenomenon occurs inside the set generated by standard Caputo IVPs; it is not merely a property of arbitrary ambient Volterra states.

# C. Same-present / opposite-basin prior art

Targeted searches were run for autonomous Caputo and Volterra bistable systems using combinations of:

- same state / different history;
- intersecting trajectories / bistability;
- history-dependent basin;
- memory-dependent attractor selection;
- opposite attractors;
- delayed tipping / rollback / hysteresis.

No publication was found meeting all strict criteria simultaneously.

## Closest source: Khalighi et al. 2026

Moein Khalighi, Chandler Ross, Ville Laitinen, Guilhem Sommeria-Klein and Leo Lahti, “Memory reshapes stability landscapes: resilience-resistance tradeoffs and critical transitions,” arXiv:2602.20365.

This preprint is a serious prior-art threat because it directly studies bistable fractional systems and reports:

- history-dependent and time-evolving stability landscapes;
- delayed collapse or recovery after stress ends;
- rebound/rollback;
- memory-broadened hysteresis;
- different histories/initializations producing different landscape states even when the observed physical state can coincide momentarily;
- ecological/biological interpretations of memory-dependent transition risk.

However, it does not supply the strict theorem requested in ROUND-0012. Its strongest transition examples involve perturbation protocols and/or time-varying forcing, and its “same state” discussion is not an autonomous standard-IVP theorem identifying two points of \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) in opposite asymptotic basins.

There is also a semantic caution: in scalar autonomous finite-terminal Caputo IVPs, Cong–Tuan separation forbids equal-time intersection of distinct standard solutions. Therefore a scalar statement that histories “momentarily share the same state” must be read in the source's actual forcing/time/history setting, not silently upgraded to a counterexample to scalar nonintersection.

### Verdict 4 — same-present opposite-basin prior art

**NO RESOLVING RESULT FOUND.**

This is a negative search result, not proof of absence.

Khalighi et al. is **CLOSE PRIOR ART** for the narrative and phenomenology and sharply raises the novelty bar.

# D. Hereditary / Volterra theorem subsumption

The broad state-space idea is classical.

## Miller–Sell

Richard K. Miller and George R. Sell, *Volterra Integral Equations and Topological Dynamics*, Memoirs AMS 102 (1970), is foundational for treating Volterra equations dynamically after state-space enlargement. Doan–Kloeden explicitly position their singular Caputo construction in this tradition.

## Weakly singular kernels

Miller–Feldstein, SIAM Journal on Mathematical Analysis 2 (1971), DOI 10.1137/0502022, treats nonlinear Volterra equations with kernels integrable at the origin even when unbounded there, including \(a(t)=t^{-p}\), \(0<p<1\). Thus weak singularity itself is not a new mathematical obstacle.

Their result concerns smoothness/regularity, not global multistable basin geometry.

## Equivalent histories / minimal states

Fabrizio–Morro 2005, DOI 10.1002/mma.581, develops equivalence classes of histories and a minimal-state viewpoint in viscoelastic memory. Conti–Marchini–Pata 2010, DOI 10.3934/dcds.2010.27.1535, develops a minimal-state framework with a regular global attractor for a viscoelastic equation.

These are important because an arbitrary full history is not automatically the minimal dynamical state.

## History-state functional in Caputo control

Gomoyunov 2020, DOI 10.1137/19M1279368, formulates a Caputo optimal-control value as a functional on a suitable history-of-motion space and proves a dynamic programming principle. This is direct evidence that future optimization/continuation for Caputo systems naturally lives on a history state, not on \(x(t)\) alone.

### What was not found

No inspected hereditary/Volterra theorem applies almost verbatim to the canonical autonomous Caputo reachable set and produces:

- a global extinction/survival basin boundary;
- a characterization of the fibers \(e_0^{-1}(x)\cap\mathcal R_\alpha\);
- a criterion determining whether one such fiber intersects two different basins;
- transfer of that structure to a positive Double-Allee system.

### Verdict 5 — hereditary/Volterra theorem subsumption

**SUBSTANTIAL PARTIAL THEORY.**

The enlarged-state formalism and substantial invariant/attractor machinery are classical/mature. The exact basin-fiber question on \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) was not found as a ready-made theorem.

# E. Markovian / diffusive-lift subsumption

Trigeassou–Maamri 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.ifacol.2024.08.201, surveys an Infinite State Representation in which a fractional integrator/system is decomposed into an infinite-dimensional family of ordinary differential modes with frequency-distributed state variables. The framework addresses initialization, fractional energy, stability analysis, and finite-dimensional approximation.

Adjacent stochastic-Volterra theory likewise uses completely monotone kernels and Hilbert-space Markovian lifts, including fractional-kernel cases.

Therefore:

> “fractional memory cannot be Markovized” is false as a novelty premise.

But Markovization does not imply that the **finite-dimensional current population vector** is a sufficient state. On the contrary, it makes the missing internal memory coordinates explicit.

No inspected diffusive-lift source supplied the strict extinction/survival basin-fiber theorem needed here.

### Verdict 6 — Markovian/diffusive-lift subsumption

**SUBSTANTIAL PARTIAL THEORY.**

The infinite-dimensional Markovian representation is mature prior art; it does not collapse the basin problem to current \(x\).

# F. Physical IVP slice versus reachable orbit set

For a standard finite-terminal Caputo model, the clean hierarchy is:

| Object | Interpretation | Physical status |
|---|---|---|
| \(\iota(X_{\rm phys})\) | constant input functions at initialization | canonical \(t=0\) physical slice |
| \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) | all states reached after finite time from that slice | strongest default physical state space for this candidate |
| \(\overline{\mathcal R_\alpha}\) | reachable states plus limiting states | natural for attractors/asymptotics |
| admissible infinite-past histories | a different initialization convention | physical only if model specifies it |
| arbitrary \(f\in\mathfrak C\) | ambient semigroup state | mathematical closure; not automatically physical |

Thus a global basin in all of \(\mathfrak C\) is not by itself the ecological object of interest.

For standard Caputo IVPs, the most defensible basin object is the restriction to \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) or, where asymptotics require closure, \(\overline{\mathcal R_\alpha}\).

# G. Basin dependence on memory within reachable states

The strongest theorem-level fact found is noninjectivity of \(e_0|_{\mathcal R_\alpha}\).

The strongest phenomenological prior found is Khalighi et al. 2026.

What remains unlocated is the bridge:

\[
\exists \phi,\psi\in\mathcal R_\alpha:
\quad
e_0(\phi)=e_0(\psi),
\quad
\phi\ne\psi,
\quad
\phi\in\mathcal B(A_1),
\quad
\psi\in\mathcal B(A_2),
\quad
A_1\ne A_2.
\]

This is the precise theorem-level statement that separates a merely non-Markovian projection from a genuinely **basin-ambiguous present state**.

No resolving result was found for it.

# H. Monotone / competitive systems as a possible killer

Modern comparison theory is strong. Cheng–Wu 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.jmaa.2026.130509, develops a complete family of Caputo system comparison principles under appropriate monotonicity assumptions.

The strongest structural killer remains narrower:

- scalar systems: distinct standard Caputo trajectories do not meet;
- triangular systems: Cong–Tuan nonintersection extends sufficiently to generate a nonlocal flow, and Doan–Kloeden show attractor behavior closely tracks the corresponding ODE under their triangular/dissipative assumptions;
- strictly ordered pairs in suitable cooperative/semiquasimonotone classes can be controlled by comparison.

However, no general theorem was found saying that every monotone or competitive multidimensional Caputo system has an injective projection \(e_0|_{\mathcal R_\alpha}\), especially for incomparable initial states.

A natural Double-Allee predator–prey model is generally not a scalar/triangular system, so the known killer does not automatically apply.

### Verdict 7 — monotone-system killer

**SUBSTANTIAL PARTIAL THEORY.**

It kills scalar/triangular formulations and constrains ordered trajectories, but no general multidimensional ecological killer was identified.

# J. Double / Multiple Allee relevance

Direct fractional Allee basin literature already exists, including the commensurate/incommensurate basin studies recorded in ROUND-0011. Therefore “fractional Double Allee has basins that depend on memory/order” is too broad to be novel.

The stronger physical interpretation remains natural:

- two population histories can in principle encode different latent memory while showing identical current prey/predator abundances;
- recovery after a perturbation may depend on accumulated history;
- extinction risk at a given observed abundance can therefore be a history-state question rather than a point-state question.

Khalighi et al. makes this biological narrative plausible and close to existing published/preprint work. What it does **not** provide is the general theorem on reachable basin fibers, nor a Double-Allee theorem realizing it.

### Verdict 8 — Double-Allee physical relevance

**CLOSE PRIOR ART.**

The phenomenon is physically natural rather than decorative, but the qualitative narrative itself can no longer carry novelty.

## Inferred gap

The residual question should be stated narrowly as:

> For an autonomous multidimensional Caputo system with a biologically admissible positive region and at least two asymptotic outcomes, characterize the fibers of the current-state evaluation
> \[
> e_0:\mathcal R_\alpha\to X_{\rm phys},
